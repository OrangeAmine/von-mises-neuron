Explain · the sign, the boundary, and the scope

The cosine addition identity fixes the minus sign on b. A positive amplitude peaks when θ + μ = 0 modulo 2π, so the preferred angle is −μ. Every individual angular input can shift by 2π without changing the core sum; adding the same bias preserves this.

At κ = 0 every response is zero and every angle ties; there is no unique preferred angle. At κ = 0.001 a formal maximum exists, but the tiny contribution makes phase a weak descriptor of the function. In the research layer κ = softplus(raw_κ): finite raw values give strictly positive amplitudes in exact arithmetic. The zero setting demonstrates the limiting function.

Affine input projections and raw-input residuals can break a composed model’s input periodicity. A preferred standardized sensor value is not evidence of a physical phase. The algebra establishes equivalent functions, not equal gradients, training speed, or generalization. Layer equations · v1 optimization findings.

Self-check: If μ increases by π, what changes? The response flips sign; the preferred direction rotates by π. Its period remains 2π.

Explain · why concentration cannot create a clock

Multiplying a cosine by κ scales its height, with no change in frequency or relative width. In the exponential, increasing κ suppresses off-center values while the peak stays one. At κ = 0 the sum is zero and the kernel is one everywhere.

For θ(t) = 2πt/T, a positive integer h gives repeated peaks every T/h hours (when κ > 0). T and h supply this structure; concentration bands do not. For h > 1 the circle embedding wraps h times, so there are multiple peaks. A noninteger h would generally break the 2π input wrap guarantee.

The Gaussian equality uses Euclidean chord distance between unit-circle embeddings, not an approximation using squared angular distance. At h = 1 and κ > 0, the corresponding density is exp(κ cos(θ − c))/(2πI₀(κ)); a peak of one alone does not normalize its integral. The unnormalized exponential in the core research layer can overflow; adding a bias after exp also removes a general positivity guarantee. Peak kernel implementation.

Self-check: With κ = 2 and θ − c = π/2 at h = 1, both kernel equations give exp(−2) ≈ 0.135335. Does that value have units of hours? No: it is dimensionless.

Explain · what searching consumes

The minimum of nested noisy development scores cannot rise when candidates are added. That selected minimum is optimistic about true risk. Because reserved residuals never enter the selector, their expected MSE stays one; finite simulated means fluctuate. More development observations reduce score noise.

Choosing again on the reserved scores would consume them for selection. You would need a fresh evaluation for that new procedure. This constructed example does not diagnose misconduct or quantify bias in locality-v2; the historical study used a separate locked confirmation stage. Real candidates can have different risks and correlated errors.
